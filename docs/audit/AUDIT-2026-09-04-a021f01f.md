## 1. Executive summary

Covantic sells parametric cover on an AI agent's USDC balance. A holder pays a premium into a
single vault; stakers back the vault and share premiums; an oracle key files claims and drives
settlement. The design's central promise, made in `CLAUDE.md` and in every proof instruction's
doc comment, is that the chain bounds each payout by a number it measured itself — a balance
drop, an overshoot past a declared envelope, a departure of account control — so that neither a
compromised oracle key nor an off-chain verdict can release funds on its own word.

**That promise holds for the arithmetic and fails for the economics.** Every proof instruction
does re-derive its bound from state the program reads (verified, §4 and §9). But the bound is
"how much left the agent's account", and on the agent-error trigger the holder controls the
agent, the envelope is derived from a history the holder writes for free, the envelope's price
is hard-coded to zero, and the payout-cannot-exceed-premium bound that justified the zero price
was removed the same day. The result is ECO-06: a holder extracts up to the full coverage for a
one-hour premium of ~0.11 USDC per 100,000 USDC, repeatably, bounded only by an off-chain hourly
breaker that defaults above the vault's balance. The project's own memory records the removal
of the premium bound as a deliberate decision; this report prices that decision.

Two structural defects sit underneath most of the rest. **A policy in `ClaimPending` has no
exit** (ANC-06): a holder-filed `submit_claim` with no evidence parks a policy there forever,
its coverage never leaves `total_coverage`, and because the unstake floor and the issuance floor
are both 50 % of coverage, two 1,000,000 USDC policies costing ~1.14 USDC each freeze every
staker's principal and halt issuance until the operator pays the attacker's claim through the
legacy instruction. Live devnet already carries 11 such policies. And **checkpoint accounts stay
writable while a claim is pending** (ANC-04, ANC-05): the permissionless cranks can displace the
only pre-incident baseline, so a valid agent-error claim (6 h lock) or governance claim (2 h
lock) reverts on chain and is recorded `failed`. The demo build's 30-second locks mask this; a
production build exhibits it on every agent-error claim with a hostile tick in a ≥4 h window.

The centralization picture is what the code says it is, with two exceptions the documentation
does not. Legacy `verify_and_payout`, which trusts the oracle's amount, is still live for all
four triggers (CEN-06) and settled 5 of the 19 paid policies on devnet. And `verify_and_payout_v2`
does not bound the amount by the guardian-signed price at all — `executed_price` and
`subject_quantity` are oracle-supplied, so the bound collapses to `coverage_amount` (ANC-09).
Upgrade authority, admin and oracle are three plain hot wallets; the deployed binary cannot be
tied to the audited commit.

### What to act on first

1. **ECO-06** — decide the agent-error economics: either reinstate a payout bound (a multiple of
   premium, not 1×), price the extractable slice (`envelope_flat_premium` is wired end to end
   and set to 0), or make the derived envelope cost something to author. Until then
   `AGENT_ERROR_PROOF_ENABLED=false` is the only control, and it is off-chain.
2. **ANC-06 with ANC-04/ANC-05** — one program upgrade: add `reject_claim` (oracle-signed, after
   the lock, releasing `total_coverage`), let `expire_policy` accept an expired `ClaimPending`
   policy, and require `STATE_ACTIVE` in both checkpoint cranks. Ship this **before** retiring
   the legacy instruction, because today a one-unit legacy payout is the only way to unstick a
   policy.
3. **CEN-06 and ANC-09** — retire `verify_and_payout` (or gate it behind an admin flag with an
   event) and give `verify_and_payout_v2` a chain-measured ceiling from the checkpoint the
   purchase already writes; require `VerificationLevel::Full`. Then the "bounded by proof" table
   in §7 becomes true for all four triggers.

### Critical

### ECO-06 — A policyholder extracts up to full coverage on the agent-error trigger for the tier premium alone

| | |
|---|---|
| **Severity** | Critical (Impact: Critical × Likelihood: Medium) |
| **Category** | Economic / Incentive |
| **Status** | Pending — the removal of the payout bound is a documented decision (`verify_and_payout_agent_error.rs:110-130`, commit `5a18be7` #58, `docs/DEMO.md`); this finding prices it |
| **Location** | `packages/api/src/services/quote-decision.ts:69-84` (envelope priced at 0) · `services/envelope-derivation.ts:74-122` (cap = 5 × p95 or = balance) · `services/agent-error/baseline.ts:63-73,148-212` (history any caller can write) · `services/agent-error/adjudicate.ts:188,213-219,308-314` · `instructions/verify_and_payout_agent_error.rs:105-135,224-262` · `instructions/create_policy.rs:101-157,245-276` · `state/risk_attestation.rs:14-67` @ `a021f01` |

**Description**

The agent-error trigger pays the amount by which a movement the agent itself signed exceeded a
declared cap. Three controls were designed to make that safe against a holder who controls the
agent; at this commit none is in force:

1. *Price the extractable slice.* `quote-decision.ts:77` hard-codes `envelopeFlatPremiumRaw = 0`
   and `routes/policies.ts` publishes 0 into the attestation. The on-chain field and its
   `<= coverage_amount` check (`create_policy.rs:140-147`) survive but always read 0.
2. *Cap the payout at the premium.* Removed by #58; `verify_and_payout_agent_error.rs:105-108`
   and `:259-262` bound the payout only by coverage and the measured overshoot. The comment at
   `:110-130` explains why. `quote-decision.ts:74-75` and `envelope-derivation.ts:21-25` still
   cite the removed bound as the reason the envelope is unpriced.
3. *Derive the envelope from history the holder cannot choose.* `deriveEnvelope` sets
   `cap = 5 × p95(outflows)` once five outflows are recorded, else `cap = current balance`
   (`envelope-derivation.ts:89-95`). Outflows of any size to any destination are recorded for any
   agent anyone asks about (`baseline.ts:64,110-119`; `routes/risk.ts`); on the balance basis the
   cap is fixed at quote time while the balance can be raised afterwards.

The mandate written by the purchase is usable immediately (`MandateMaturity::Immediate`,
`create_policy.rs:254`), the purchase writes the baseline checkpoint (`:266-276`), and the
attestation does not bind coverage (ANC-08), so nothing on either side waits or checks capital.

**Exploit path** (balance basis; the history basis differs only in step 2)

1. Attacker holds holder key H, agent key A with a USDC ATA holding 1 USDC, and a third wallet W.
2. `POST /api/policies/quote {coverage: 1 USDC, duration: 3600, agent: A}`. No history → cap =
   balance = 1 USDC (`envelope-derivation.ts:89-95`); `maxInsurableCoverageRaw` = 1 USDC passes
   (`quote-decision.ts:47-60`). The oracle publishes the attestation with `mandate_hash(cap = 1
   USDC)`, `envelope_flat_premium = 0`, valid 3,600 s.
3. H calls `create_policy(coverage_amount = 100,000 USDC, duration 3600, A, mandate)` directly
   — the attestation carries no coverage (ANC-08). Premium at LOW: 1e11 × 100 / 10⁴ × 3,600 /
   31,536,000 = 114,155 base units = **0.114 USDC** (MEDIUM 0.285, HIGH 0.571). Checkpoint
   amount = 1 USDC (`create_policy.rs:266-276`).
4. H deposits 100,000 USDC into A's ATA. The next `checkpoint_balance` tick (the protocol's own
   sweep, or anyone) records 100,001 USDC (`checkpoint_balance.rs:84-99`).
5. A signs a plain SPL transfer of 100,000 USDC to W. The sweep flags `mandate_envelope_exceeded`;
   the next tick pins the 100,001 reading (`checkpoint_balance.rs:69-75`).
6. `adjudicateAgentError`: A was the signer and there is no foreign authority (`adjudicate.ts:188`
   passes); W is neither holder nor agent so `allDestinationsSelf` is false (`:213-219` passes);
   the USDC loss prices; `payable = min(excess 99,999, loss)` capped to coverage (`:308-314`).
   Confidence ≈ 0.82 → `pay` lane with chain proof (`confidence-lanes.ts`); breaker permits up to
   100,000 USDC/h (`env.ts:230-232`). `oracle_submit_claim` lands.
7. After `LOCK_AGENT_ERROR` (21,600 s) `verify_and_payout_agent_error`: baseline = pin 100,001
   (`:184-197`), current 1 → `observed_drop` 100,000; `cap_excess` 99,999 ≥ 1 USDC; `payout ≤
   99,999` (`:225-262`). The vault transfers **99,999 USDC to H**.
8. Attacker holds 100,000 (in W) + 99,999 − 0.114. Repeatable per policy; the history variant
   (five 1-base-unit transfers A→H, cap = 5 base units) needs no deposit step.

The on-chain half is reproduced executably (§8, `INV-CKPT-01 (agent-error, pre-claim inflation)`):
against a production build, deposit 20,000 → `checkpoint_balance` → withdraw pays 20,000 USDC on a
100-USDC agent with zero real loss. The chain measures a movement, not a loss; only the pricing and
the off-chain gates above could have distinguished them, and none does on this trigger.

**Impact**

Up to the full coverage per cycle for a premium six orders of magnitude smaller; on the live
devnet vault (36,493 USDC staked) one cycle takes the whole staker principal. On mainnet the loss
rate is `min(vault, AUTO_PAYOUT_HOURLY_LIMIT_RAW)` per hour with the breaker on (default 100,000
USDC/h, above today's vault) and unbounded with it off or Redis unreadable (CEN-10). The 6-hour
lock is the only window, and the claim looks by construction exactly like the covered event.
Payout/premium for a one-hour policy: LOW 876,000×, MEDIUM 350,400×, HIGH 175,200×.

**Recommendation**

None of these closes it alone; together they turn a free option into a priced one.
(a) Bound the payout at N × premium on this trigger only (N ≈ 50–200) — the 1× bound was
rightly called a deposit; a multiple keeps cover meaningful while capping the self-dealing return.
(b) Restore a non-zero `envelope_flat_premium` at an actuarial rate on `(coverage − cap)`; the
field is wired end to end. (c) Make the record cost something: exclude outflows to the
holder/agent family and below a dust floor from the p95, require aged history (e.g. ≥ 7 days,
≥ 20 observations) before the history basis, and on the balance basis re-read the balance at
purchase or floor the cap at `k × coverage` (k ≈ 0.2–0.3) inside `deriveEnvelope` (the hash binds
it; `validate_mandate` only requires `cap > 0`). (d) Bind coverage into the attestation (ANC-08)
so the quote's balance bound is real. (e) Put the breaker on chain (CEN-10).

### Major

### ANC-04 — Checkpoint accounts stay writable while a claim is pending, so permissionless ticks can erase the only baseline a valid agent-error or governance claim can be paid against

| | |
|---|---|
| **Severity** | Major (Impact: Medium × Likelihood: High) |
| **Category** | Logical Issue |
| **Status** | Pending |
| **Location** | `instructions/checkpoint_balance.rs:35-39` (admits `ClaimPending`), `:59-65` (`pin_expired` against `now`), `:76-90` (expired pin replaced by the post-drop reading) · `instructions/verify_and_payout_agent_error.rs:137-141` (6 h lock), `:184-227` (baseline selection, `DropBelowMinimum`) · `instructions/checkpoint_authority.rs:46-50`, `:60-69` (single unpinned `prev_*`) · `instructions/verify_and_payout_governance.rs:141-155` @ `a021f01` |

**Description**

Both cranks are permissionless by design and both accept a `ClaimPending` policy. The payout
instructions keep one level of history (`prev_*`) to survive a tick that lands between the
incident and the claim. That history does not survive ticks that land *after* the claim:

- *Balance path.* `checkpoint_balance` holds the pre-drop pin only until
  `now − prev_unix_timestamp > MAX_CHECKPOINT_AGE` (7,200 s, `:61-65`); once expired, the else
  branch at `:84-90` overwrites `prev_*` with the current post-drop reading. The agent-error lock
  is `LOCK_AGENT_ERROR = 21,600` s (`:137-141`), so on every production agent-error claim there is
  a window of at least four hours in which one call to `checkpoint_balance` — from anyone — turns
  the baseline into the post-drop figure. At payout `checkpoint.unix_timestamp >
  claim_submitted_at` selects `prev_*` (`:188-197`), `observed_drop` is 0 and the instruction
  reverts `DropBelowMinimum` (`:225-227`). The exploit path is safe by arithmetic: its 1 h lock
  and 2 h staleness share the reference point, so the pin cannot expire before the payout window
  closes.
- *Authority path.* `checkpoint_authority` has no pin at all: `prev_*` is always the previous
  reading (`:60-69`). Two calls after the claim push the pre-claim reading out; the governance
  payout then finds `prev_unix_timestamp > claim_submitted_at` and reverts
  `AuthorityCheckpointOutOfWindow` (`verify_and_payout_governance.rs:152-155`) — unconditionally,
  even for a pure seizure where the balance is only needed for `observed_drop`.

- *Exploit path, shape B.* The `dropped` branch re-pins on every fall (`checkpoint_balance.rs:59,
  68-74`), so a second movement after the claim plus ticks before and after it displaces the
  pre-claim pin; the payout then finds a post-claim baseline and reverts `CheckpointOutOfWindow`.

The protocol's own sweep selects `PolicyState.Active` only (`workers/exploit-watcher.ts:189`), so
in production this needs a hostile caller or the ≤60 s indexer-lag edge. **All three shapes were
reproduced executably** against a production build (§8, `INV-CKPT-02`): the control arm pays, the
same claim with the extra tick(s) reverts. The keeper records a
non-`LockPeriodNotElapsed` revert as `failed`, a terminal status, and the policy stays
`ClaimPending` forever (ANC-06). No test covers a post-claim tick.

**Exploit path**

1. Holder V has a policy on agent X with a matured mandate. X moves more than the cap at t₁;
   the sweep pins the pre-drop balance A at t₂ and files the claim at t₃ (all within minutes;
   the pin timestamp t₀ ≤ t₁).
2. Anyone calls `checkpoint_balance(X's policy)` at t₄ ∈ (t₀ + 7,200 s, t₃ + 21,600 s). The
   window is ≥ 4 h wide and the call costs a transaction fee. `prev_*` becomes (B, t₂), the
   post-drop reading.
3. At t₃ + 21,600 s the keeper sends `verify_and_payout_agent_error`. Baseline = (B, t₂);
   `observed_drop = B − B = 0`; revert `DropBelowMinimum`. The keeper records `failed`.
4. V's valid claim of up to coverage is denied; V's policy is `ClaimPending` with no exit; the
   vault keeps V's coverage in `total_coverage`. Attacker spends ~0.000005 SOL and gains nothing
   directly — denial of coverage, and a lever against a competitor's or an insurer's book.

**Impact**

Wrongful denial of every production agent-error claim a hostile party chooses to target, and of
every governance claim targeted with two calls in its 2 h lock, followed by the ANC-06 lock-in.
The demo build (`devnet-fast-lock`, 30 s locks) never shows it, which is why the devnet history
of paid agent-error claims is not evidence against it. The bankrun reproduction (§8) shows the
control claim paying 1 USDC and the identical claim reverting `DropBelowMinimum` after one tick.

**Recommendation**

One change closes both variants: `require!(policy.state == STATE_ACTIVE)` in `checkpoint_balance`
and `checkpoint_authority`. Every payout re-reads the covered account live for the "after" side,
so a post-claim checkpoint has no legitimate purpose. Alternatively stop advancing `prev_*` while
`ClaimPending`, and measure the balance pin's expiry against `claim_submitted_at` when set.
Add a bankrun test: claim, tick after the pin ages 2 h, warp past the lock, payout succeeds.

**Alleviation** — empty.

### ANC-05 — The authority checkpoint has no pre-drop pin, so a seizure followed by a drain is measured from the post-drain reading whenever an ordinary tick lands before the claim

| | |
|---|---|
| **Severity** | Major (Impact: Medium × Likelihood: High) |
| **Category** | Logical Issue |
| **Status** | Pending |
| **Location** | `instructions/checkpoint_authority.rs:60-69` · `instructions/verify_and_payout_governance.rs:141-150,204-233` · compare `instructions/checkpoint_balance.rs:44-90` (the fix that was applied to the balance path) @ `a021f01` |

**Description**

`checkpoint_balance` pins the reading that preceded a drop so that a crank tick between the
incident and the claim does not overwrite the only usable "before" (`checkpoint_balance.rs:44-58`
documents the bug this fixed). `checkpoint_authority` never received the same fix: `prev_*` is
simply the previous reading (`:60-69`). The governance payout takes the current reading as its
baseline whenever `checkpoint.unix_timestamp <= claim_submitted_at` (`:141-143`) and computes
`observed_drop = baseline_amount − current_amount` (`:205`). For a takeover that also drains,
one ordinary sweep tick after the drain and before the claim records the post-drain balance as
the baseline; `observed_drop` is 0, `seized_amount` is the (empty) current balance,
`max_provable_loss` is 0 and the instruction reverts `PayoutExceedsProvenGovernanceLoss`
(`:226-233`). The sweep runs every couple of minutes and the claim is filed only after off-chain
verification, so a tick in that window is the common case, not the rare one.

**Exploit path** (no attacker needed; the protocol's own crank causes it)

1. Holder V has a matured governance baseline. Attacker Z seizes X's ATA via
   `SetAuthority(AccountOwner)` and drains it to 0 in the same or the next transaction. Balance
   before: 30,000 USDC.
2. The sweep ticks: `checkpoint_authority` records `owner = Z, amount = 0`, `prev_amount =
   30,000`. Off-chain verification confirms the takeover; `oracle_submit_claim` lands afterwards.
3. After the 2 h lock: `checkpoint.unix_timestamp <= claim_submitted_at` → baseline =
   `(amount = 0, …)`; `observed_drop = 0`; `seized_amount = current_amount = 0`;
   `max_provable_loss = 0 < MIN_PROVABLE_GOVERNANCE_LOSS` → revert. The keeper records `failed`.
4. V's 30,000 USDC covered loss is denied and the policy is stuck `ClaimPending` (ANC-06).

**Impact**

The governance trigger cannot settle the seize-and-drain shape — the one its own design comment
calls "the case that matters most" (`verify_and_payout_governance.rs:197-203`) — under the normal
cadence of the crank. Pure seizures without a drain still settle (`seized_amount = current`).

**Recommendation**

Port the pin from `checkpoint_balance.rs:59-90` into `checkpoint_authority`: when
`covered.amount < checkpoint.amount`, pin the previous reading into `prev_*` and hold it while
higher and within the window; have `verify_and_payout_governance` prefer the pin under the same
`pin_usable` rule as the other two paths (and fix ANC-13 in all three at once). Then refuse the
crank on `ClaimPending` (ANC-04). Test: seize, drain, one tick, claim, payout = drop.

**Alleviation** — empty.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Severity** | Major (Impact: High × Likelihood: Medium) — see §5 for the arbitration; one specialist filed Critical |
| **Category** | Design Issue |
| **Status** | Pending — live devnet already holds 11 policies in `ClaimPending` (6,900 USDC of coverage) with no path out |
| **Location** | `instructions/submit_claim.rs:20-45` (holder, any trigger, any 1–88 bytes) · `instructions/oracle_submit_claim.rs:50-53` · `instructions/expire_policy.rs:17-20` and `instructions/cancel_policy.rs:18-21` (both require `STATE_ACTIVE`) · `lib.rs:15-279` (no reject/withdraw instruction among 27) · `ZeroPayout` guards `verify_and_payout.rs:45`, `_v2.rs:90`, `_exploit.rs:91`, `_governance.rs:105`, `_agent_error.rs:135` · `instructions/unstake.rs:87-105` (50 % floor) · `instructions/create_policy.rs:93-95` @ `a021f01` |

**Description**

stuck policy's coverage is reserved forever. Two floors turn that reservation into a freeze:
`execute_unstake` pays out only `total_staked − ceil(total_coverage × 0.5)` (`unstake.rs:87-105`;
the comment at `:83-85` assumes coverage always expires), and `create_policy` refuses new cover

payout of one base unit through legacy `verify_and_payout` — paying the claimant.

**Exploit path**

1. Vault has `total_staked = S`. Attacker quotes any agent (0.001 USDC of coverage suffices; the
   attestation does not bind coverage — ANC-08) and calls `create_policy` for 1,000,000 USDC at

3. `reserve_required = ceil(total_coverage × 0.5) ≥ S` → `free_capital = 0` → every
   `execute_unstake` reverts `SolvencyTooLow`; `solvency_ratio < 5000` with `total_coverage > 0` →
   every `create_policy` reverts `SolvencyTooLow`.
4. Attacker spends ≈ 2.3 USDC plus rent; every staker's principal (S) is frozen and the protocol
   cannot write cover until the oracle key pays a one-unit legacy payout on each attacker policy

**Impact**

Total, indefinite freeze of staker withdrawals and issuance at negligible cost; partial freezes
(C/2 of principal per C of stuck coverage) at any smaller scale. In the honest pipeline, every
claim whose settlement reverts also destroys the holder's cover for the policy's remaining term
and their premium. Live devnet shows the honest version: 11 stuck policies.

**Recommendation**

Add `reject_claim` — oracle-signed, after the lock, recording a `bundle_hash` and reason, returning
the policy to `Active` (or a terminal `Rejected` that releases `total_coverage`) and emitting an

`expire_policy` accept a `ClaimPending` policy once `expiry_time + max lock` has passed, releasing
coverage while keeping the claim record. Reconsider holder-signed `submit_claim` now that every
trigger is oracle-verified; at minimum require a matured declaration or a fee. Ship before
retiring the legacy instruction (CEN-06). The unused `ClaimAlreadySubmitted` error variant is the
one this transition needs.

**Alleviation** — empty.

**Design observation (not a finding).** Under SPL Token, a transfer out of an account the agent
still owns can be signed only by the owner or by a delegate the owner approved. The exploit
trigger's covered event — "an authority that is not the agent moved the agent's funds" — is
therefore, on the agent's own ATA, always an agent-approved delegate or an owner change (a
governance event). The provenance scan in ECO-04 reconstructs from history a fact the SPL
invariant already gives for free, and the residue (the 100-signature window) exists only because
of that choice. Deciding consent from the invariant closes ECO-04 without a history read; it also
means the exploit trigger, as scoped today, covers delegate abuse by programs the agent approved —
which is what a DeFi exploit usually looks like — only if the team decides that such abuse is a
covered event. That is a product decision this report cannot make.

## 13. Limitations

What this audit does not establish:

- **The deployed binary is not the audited code.** The live devnet program was deployed 17 minutes
  before the HEAD commit, its bytes differ from the local build, and it is a `devnet-fast-lock`
  build (35 s locks, 60 s maturity). Every finding is against the source at `a021f01`; the live
  program was used only to read state.
- **Formal verification is partial.** §8 lists which invariants have an executable, mutation-
  checked proof (solvency, conservation on the measuring paths, authorization, substitution, state
  machine, premium arithmetic, post-claim checkpoint monotonicity) and which are asserted only
  (governance and v2 bounds not separately mutated; v2 exercised against a fabricated price account,
  never through a guardian-signed update; INV-DET / INV-THREE / the confidence ceiling rest on the
  existing contract suites). Property tests sample; they do not prove absence outside the
  generators' ranges.
- **No exploit was executed against devnet.** Every exploit path in §4 was derived from the code and
  constants at the cited lines and, for ECO-06, corroborated by the project's own devnet run of the
  same shape (`docs/DEMO.md`); none was run end to end by the auditors.
- **Tools not run:** `cargo-deny` (unmaintained crates, licences, bans), `solana-verify`
  (reproducible build), `trivy` (image layers). `osv-scanner` did not receive `Cargo.lock` in the
  sweep (run separately by the dependency reviewer); `semgrep` did not fully parse
  `scripts/init-devnet.sh`; the `tsc` sweep stopped at the first failing package (the others were
  re-run individually: 0 errors). The SBF target was not installed for the host `cargo`, so the
  on-chain-vs-host split of the RustSec warnings was reasoned from `cargo tree -i`, not from a
  target build graph.
- **Out of scope:** `packages/web/src` beyond its IDL copy; the API's general OWASP surface; detection
  recall; `covantic-solana-sdk`.
- **Not verified:** whether nginx's proxy buffering alters the fragment pattern behind DEP-06;
  whether either live devnet staker position predates the loss-index upgrade (ANC-11); the custody
  of the upgrade-authority and admin keys (read from chain and operator notes, not inspected);
  Pyth receiver semantics were read from the SDK 2.0.0 source and the live receiver Config, not from
  the deployed receiver binary.
- **Engagement tree.** `docs/audit/SCOPE.md` was regenerated by the scope script;
  `packages/anchor/package.json` and `pnpm-lock.yaml` gained `fast-check` and
  `packages/anchor/tests/audit-invariants.test.ts` was added by the formal-verification pass (§8);
  no program source, API source or existing test was modified by the engagement (verified by
  SHA-256 against `SCOPE.md` at the end of each review).
- **Severity is a judgement.** The arbitrations in §5 show both axes for every disputed finding;
  a reader who weighs the 1.14 USDC cost of becoming a policyholder as "no special position" reaches
  Critical for ANC-06.

## Appendix A — Definitions

**Impact.** Critical: vault drained, arbitrary payout, upgrade or oracle authority compromised.
High: bounded but material theft; a holder extracts more than they paid; permanent freeze of user
funds. Medium: griefing at cost; a claim resolves wrongly in one direction; temporary DoS of the
claim pipeline. Low: information disclosure, recoverable misbehaviour, self-healing accounting.

**Likelihood.** High: anonymous caller, one transaction, no special position or timing. Medium:
needs to be a policyholder or staker, or a specific reachable state. Low: needs operator or key
compromise, validator-level control, or a narrow race.

| | Critical | High | Medium | Low |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **High** | Critical | Critical | Major | Minor |
| **Medium** | Critical | Major | Medium | Minor |
| **Low** | Major | Medium | Minor | Informational |

**Categories.** Centralization / Privilege · Logical Issue · Volatile Code · Data Flow · Design
Issue · Economic / Incentive · Dependency · Inconsistency · Coding Style.

**Status.** Pending · Acknowledged · Resolved (re-derived against the fix by the remediation
reviewer) · Partially Resolved · Mitigated · Declined · Withdrawn.
